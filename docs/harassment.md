# Experimental attack and withdrawal

`harasskodachi` is enabled by default. `disable=harasskodachi` disables new Kodachi sorties. `harasswide` is disabled by default; `enable=harasswide` admits Blitz, Bolas, Cyclops, Minotaur, Ravager, Dominatrix, Scorcher and Pyro to the same judge. The flags are independent. These are tactical eligibility flags, not production changes.

A candidate acts alone in a small local encounter. The planner compares immediate withdrawal with Fight or targeted Attack followed by withdrawal after the first shot. It requires a predicted return to a fixed endpoint 500 elmos behind the starting point, no more than 5% raw predicted death probability, and range clearance in both ordinary enemy UnitAI and committed pursuit branches. It chooses positive worst-branch improvement over immediate withdrawal. Kills count at metal value; surviving partial damage has a separate 10% proportional credit. These raw probabilities are not calibrated guarantees. The conservative judge rejects some successful two-Scorcher maneuvers from the original synthetic experiment.

Known guns and contacts around the approach and exit are checked; underwater routes, unreachable routes, substantial path detours, unknown local contacts and larger encounters are rejected. The planner makes at most one admission query per six frames, with six simultaneous sorties and a 120-frame per-unit retry interval. It uses frame-based round-robin scheduling, not wall-clock budgets.

Execution borrows command authority without changing the unit's parent role. The actual firing event starts the Move immediately; observed reload advancement is a fallback. Losing the target, taking 20% of starting health in damage or exceeding the approach deadline also starts withdrawal. Firing remains enabled. Arrival holds the exit until the 20-second forecast horizon, then restores ordinary control. An operator reservation, new role or stronger lease wins. Turning either flag off stops admissions but preserves existing withdrawals and their deadlines.

Sorties, phase, target, endpoint, reload observations, lease identity, retry deadlines and scheduling cursor use `checkpoint::state`. A lease is resolved using the current callback against the authority table. Non-default state round trips and continuation of an active exit are tested. This WIP branch's existing whole-bot checkpoint support is a separate integration; these tests do not claim engine save/load continuity.

Journal kind `harassment` records `start`, `withdraw` and `end`, including target, selected order, score advantage, shot timing and exit. The original engine experiment used full-health Kodachis against Scorchers/Bolas on flat ground. The wider roster is unvalidated and opt-in. Turn acceleration, projectile travel time and unforeseen reinforcements remain predictor limitations.
